# Pre-Conflict Co-Regulation: Halting Diffuse Arousal

In relational conflict, human beings rarely argue about facts; they react to autonomic threat cues. 

Pioneering research by Dr. John Gottman demonstrates that when heart rate exceeds **100 beats per minute** during conflict, an individual enters **Diffuse Physiological Arousal (DPA)**. In DPA, the capacity to process social nuance, empathy, and active listening drops to near zero, leaving only primitive fight, flight, or defensive stonewalling.

Stephen Porges' Polyvagal Theory explains that until the ventral vagus nerve stabilizes cardiac output, genuine interpersonal communication is biologically impossible.

### Step 1: Detect Relational Threat Posture
Notice what your body is doing in anticipation or aftermath of relational friction: jaw clenching, shallow chest breathing, or hollow stomach sinking.

<SomaticCheckIn />

### Step 2: Biological Ventral Vagal Anchor
Slow down cardiac output to bring your pulse safely below the DPA threshold. 4-second box breathing triggers steady respiratory sinus arrhythmia, unlocking your facial micro-muscles and prosodic vocal tone.

<BoxBreathing duration="180s" cycles="6" />

### Step 3: Vault Vulnerability & Boundary Script
Before speaking with the other person, distinguish between your defensive ego armor and your core unmet need. 

Formulate an assertive, non-adversarial boundary statement that can be delivered without defensive activation.

<VaultReflection prompt="What primitive fear is being triggered (e.g., abandonment, disrespect, invalidation)? What is your clean, non-aggressive need in this interaction?" />

### Step 4: Social Engagement Calibration
When you enter the conversation, soften your gaze, relax your vocal cords, and pause 2 full seconds before responding to any statement. You are now co-regulating the room rather than being hijacked by it.
